# Coral Reef Bleaching: Causes, Recovery, and Remaining Uncertainties

## Summary

- **Coral bleaching occurs when heat stress causes corals to expel symbiotic algae**, reducing energy production and risking mortality within weeks to months if conditions don't improve.
- **Ocean warming is the primary driver**, with secondary stressors including acidification, pollution, and disease accelerating decline in already-stressed reefs.
- **Recovery is possible but uncertain**, some reefs show resilience after bleaching events, while others face permanent degradation; outcomes depend heavily on local conditions and human management.

## Background: What Is Coral Bleaching?

Corals are animals that form partnerships with microscopic algae living in their tissues. These algae perform photosynthesis, providing up to 90% of the coral's energy needs. When water temperature rises even 1-2°C above normal seasonal levels for several weeks, corals experience thermal stress. In response, they expel their algal partners, a survival mechanism, though counterintuitive. Without the algae, corals turn white (hence "bleached") and lose their primary food source. If temperature returns to normal within a few weeks, corals can reabsorb algae and survive. If stress persists, corals starve and die.

## Key Findings

1. **Global ocean temperatures have risen ~1.1°C since pre-industrial times, directly correlating with increased bleaching frequency and severity.**
   *Importance: Warming is measurable, ongoing, and the single largest threat to reef survival globally.*

2. **The 2016 bleaching event killed approximately 30% of corals on the Great Barrier Reef; the 2020 event affected another 25% of surviving corals.**
   *Importance: Repeated bleaching within years prevents full recovery and indicates accelerating stress cycles.*

3. **Reefs in cooler, deeper waters or those protected from fishing show better recovery trajectories after bleaching.**
   *Importance: Local management can buffer climate impacts, but cannot eliminate them without global emissions reductions.*

4. **Ocean acidification, caused by increased CO₂ absorption, weakens coral skeletons and reduces calcification rates by 10-30%, compounding thermal stress.**
   *Importance: Even if warming slows, acidification remains a persistent long-term threat.*

5. **Resilient reefs often have high genetic diversity, low disease prevalence, and abundant herbivorous fish maintaining algae balance.**
   *Importance: Biodiversity and ecosystem function directly affect recovery capacity.*

## Open Questions

- How much thermal tolerance can coral populations develop through adaptation or natural selection over coming decades?
- What combination of local interventions (marine protected areas, fisheries management, coral restoration) measurably improves outcomes at reef-scale?
- Can assisted adaptation techniques (selective breeding, probiotics) be deployed effectively without unintended ecological consequences?
- How do interactions between bleaching, disease, and other stressors differ across reef regions?
