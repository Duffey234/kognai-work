     # Coral Reef Bleaching: Causes, Recovery, and Remaining Uncertainties
     
     ## Summary
     
     - **Coral bleaching occurs when heat stress causes corals to expel symbiotic algae**, reducing energy production and risking mortality within weeks to months if conditions don't improve.
     - **Ocean warming is the primary driver**, with secondary stressors including acidification, pollution, and disease accelerating decline in already-stressed reefs.
     - **Recovery is possible but uncertain**,some reefs show resilience after bleaching events, while others face permanent degradation; outcomes depend heavily on local conditions and human management.
     
     ## Background: What Is Coral Bleaching?
     
     Corals are animals that form partnerships with microscopic algae living in their tissues. These algae perform photosynthesis, providing up to 90% of the coral's energy needs. When water temperature rises even 1-2°C above normal seasonal levels for several weeks, corals experience thermal stress. In response, they expel their algal partners,a survival mechanism, though counterintuitive. Without the algae, corals turn white (hence "bleached") and lose their primary food source. If temperature returns to normal within a few weeks, corals can reabsorb algae and survive. If stress persists, corals starve and die.

     1. **Global ocean temperatures have risen ~1.1°C since pre-industrial times, directly correlating with increased bleaching frequency and severity.**
